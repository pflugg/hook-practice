Video transcript and visual descriptions
  1. 0–8 seconds · First-game nerves. “Practice privately before you play with people.” Original geometric players sit around a table. “New game night. Same first-turn nerves?” Give yourself a place to try, ask why, and start again. No pressure. Repeat any step.
  2. 8–16 seconds · See the next move. “Move to a different unlocked location before using its actions.” A pawn moves from Ship to Rock, then Gain 1 becomes highlighted. This is a simplified lesson diagram.
  3. 16–24 seconds · Understand why. “Need 4 Power? Gain 1 before paying for the Map.” The Power count changes from 3 to 4 beside a Map costing 4. The explanation connects gaining Power to unlocking the route.
  4. 24–32 seconds · Practise a decision. “Check the total before a clash: 4 + 4 = 8. Equal Strength works.” Two crew cards each show 4 against a Hero showing 8. Selected crew are spent afterward. Follow along first; choose a plan when you want to.
  5. 32–40 seconds · See the plan. “Six guided lessons. Three focused drills. A coached simulated voyage.” Three illustrated panels explain Coach (a legal next click), Why this move (the reason for a choice), and Strategy brief (objective, progress, next steps).
  6. 40–48 seconds · Join the table with confidence. “A teaching companion for learning before game night.” Neverland Duel. Start with Tutorial. Independent teaching prototype. Current lesson: Captain Hook’s objective in Disney Villainous. Solo adaptations are explained before table play. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Disney or Ravensburger.
